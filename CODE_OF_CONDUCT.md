# Code of conduct

This is a small project with one maintainer, so this document is short and says
what is actually true rather than what sounds thorough.

## What is expected

Argue about the work. Disagreement about a design decision, a threat model entry,
a wording choice or a proof is the point of an open specification, and a
well-argued "this is wrong because X" is the most useful thing anyone can bring
here — including when it lands hard.

What is not welcome is the other thing: personal attacks, harassment, demeaning
or discriminatory remarks, unwelcome attention, publishing someone's private
information, or persistent disruption after being asked to stop. None of that is
a grey area, and none of it becomes acceptable because the technical point
underneath it happened to be correct.

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any
other space that carries this project's name.

## Reporting

Email `bernalli@proton.me`. Reports are read by one person — the maintainer — and
are not shared further without the reporter's agreement, except where the law
requires it.

Be honest about what that means in practice: there is no committee, no appeals
process, and no guaranteed response time. What there is, is someone who will read
it, take it seriously, and act.

## Enforcement

Depending on what happened, the response ranges from a private request to stop,
to editing or removing the content, to blocking the account from the repository.
Serious or repeated behaviour goes straight to the last one.

Security vulnerabilities are not a conduct matter and follow a different path —
see [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md), and do not open a public issue for one.
